Mora SP Cup 2026 — Solution Report

Low-Light Image Denoising & Enhancement Challenge
Team: Noice Floor Ninjas  |  Preliminary Round

1. Introduction

Low-light photography produces images heavily corrupted by read noise, thermal noise, and shot noise. The Mora SP Cup 2026 provides 460 paired noisy/clean images (992×992 PNG, RGB) generated via a synthetic low-light noise model. Our task: design a denoising solution that removes these artifacts while preserving image details and structure. Our team designed a three-stage hybrid pipeline combining classical signal processing (adaptive defect correction, wavelet denoising, bilateral filtering) with a NAFNet deep learning denoiser, achieving a composite score of 0.643 — a 2.44× improvement over the NLM baseline (0.263).

Design rationale: Stage 1 removes ~8,758 extreme outlier pixels before they corrupt CNN convolutions. Stage 2 suppresses high-frequency noise (power ratio = 25.43) via wavelet thresholding, reducing the noise floor for the DL model. Stage 3 (NAFNet) learns the non-linear residual mapping from the 420-image training set. Stage 4 applies edge-preserving smoothing to suppress residual flat-region noise.

NAFNet choice: Selected for its state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM with efficient SimpleGate + Simplified Channel Attention (no expensive self-attention), enabling practical CPU inference. The U-Net encoder-decoder structure with skip connections and a global residual connection (input + predicted residual) handles multi-scale noise patterns.

4. Methodology

4.1 Classical Signal Processing

Adaptive Defect Correction: Multi-scale median filter (kernels [3,5]) with adaptive thresholds: T = base + 2×local_std, where local_std is estimated via Gaussian-weighted variance (15×15 window). Adapts to regions with varying noise intensity, improving over the baseline's fixed 0.25 threshold.

Wavelet Denoising (BayesShrink): db4 wavelet, 2-level decomposition per RGB channel. Noise σ estimated via MAD: σ̂ = median(|d|)/0.6745 from finest detail coefficients. Adaptive per-subband thresholds: T = σ̂²/√(σ_d²−σ̂²). Soft thresholding suppresses high-frequency noise while preserving edges and texture.

Bilateral Filtering: Post-processing with conservative d=5, σ_color=25, σ_space=25. The dual spatial/intensity proximity weighting smooths flat regions while strictly preserving edge gradients.

Frequency-Domain Analysis: DFT-based power spectral density analysis and Butterworth low-pass filtering for noise characterization. Wiener filtering (optimal MMSE estimator) also implemented for comparison. These informed our pipeline design rather than serving as standalone denoisers.

6. Validation Approach

Hold-out split: 420 training / 40 validation images (91%/9%). Validation set (images 421–460) never used during training. The official composite score formula was integrated directly into the training loop:

Composite = 0.6 × clip(ΔPSNR/15, 0, 1) + 0.4 × max(ΔSSIM, 0)   where ΔPSNR = PSNR_denoised − PSNR_noisy

SSIM parameters match the official evaluator exactly (win_size=7, gaussian_weights=False, K1=0.01, K2=0.03). Independent evaluation on the full 460-image public set uses the provided evaluation/evaluate.py script. A verify_submission.py script validates the 20 output files for correct naming (461.png–480.png), dimensions (992×992), format (RGB PNG), and count.

Our pipeline achieves 0.643 composite (2.44× baseline), with ΔPSNR of +10.75 dB and ΔSSIM of +0.413, demonstrating substantial gains in both pixel-level accuracy and structural fidelity. Inference: ~0.74 s/image (CUDA T4 GPU), ~4.3 s/image (CPU) for full 992×992 images without tiling.

Per-stage contribution: Defect correction prevents localized CNN artifacts from ~8,758 outliers. Wavelet pre-processing reduces the high-frequency noise floor. NAFNet provides the primary non-linear denoising. Bilateral post-filter refines edge quality.

8. Limitations

  • Training data: 420 pairs is modest; more data or pre-training could improve generalization.
  • Single model: No test-time augmentation (TTA) or ensemble due to GPU time constraints.
  • Fixed parameters: Classical stage parameters are fixed; per-image adaptive tuning could yield gains.
  • Loss function: Pure L1; adding SSIM loss would directly optimize the competition metric.
  • Hidden GT: Submission images 461–480 have no ground truth; score estimated from public set.

9. Conclusion

We presented a hybrid classical-DL denoising pipeline combining adaptive defect correction, BayesShrink wavelet denoising, NAFNet, and bilateral filtering. The approach achieves a composite score of 0.643 (2.44× over baseline 0.263) with practical inference on GPU (~0.74 s) and CPU (~4.3 s). Four classical SP techniques are integrated beyond the baseline. For the final round, improvements include TTA, SSIM-aware loss, and ensembling.
